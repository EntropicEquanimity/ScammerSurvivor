# Scam realism 2026

Research pass, 16 Oct 2026. Goal: make the cards read like the scams people in the eight packs get today.

Method. Perplexity found sources. Each claim below was then opened at the primary source (police, regulator, anti-scam centre, or a state news agency quoting one). A claim not opened at a primary is marked **not verified**. Perplexity is never cited. Tactics only: nothing here is message text, and the few short quoted phrases come from our own live cards, not from scams.

Packs are listed in alphabetical order. No pack leads.

## 1. What scammers do now, per country

### AU

Source: ACCC media release on the National Anti-Scam Centre Targeting Scams Report 2025, 30 Mar 2026. https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/continued-action-critical-to-combat-fraud-as-annual-scam-losses-exceed-2-billion

- Combined 2025 losses AUD 2.18b from 274,577 loss reports. Top five by loss: investment 837.7m, payment redirection 166.8m, romance 139.9m, phishing 97.6m, remote access 69.9m.
- Phishing was the most reported scam to Scamwatch (65,361). Text-message scam reports fell from 77,365 to 29,058. Online contact (websites, social media) with a loss rose 31.8%.
- The ACCC names AI and scam compounds as the drivers of rising sophistication. No AI mechanic is described in the release.
- Betting and sports "investment" (scambling) losses nearly tripled.

Mechanics, from Scamwatch alerts (opened in a browser):
- **Investment**, 28 Sep 2026, https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/about-us/news-and-alerts/scam-alert-investment-scams-hiding-in-plain-sight. Channel: a hobby chat group (fishing, sport) the victim is added to. A "professor" promotes a platform once the group feels friendly. Other members post testimonials and returns. Variants: a referral from a trusted friend who sees fake profits, and fake news pages that claim a platform passed checks or show a celebrity in a made-up story. Small withdrawals are allowed early. Big withdrawals need a fee or tax.
- **Fake purchase callback**, 14 Jul 2026, https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/about-us/news-and-alerts/scam-alert-fake-purchase-callback-scams. Channel: text, email, app notification, calendar invite or iMessage. Opener: a receipt for something you did not buy, often AUD 300 to 2,000 (phone, software renewal, tickets, crypto), sometimes with your real name and address. There is no link. The ask is to call the printed number before the money leaves. On the call: bank or card details "for the refund", an "overpayment" to send back, a remote-access install, or gift cards to "help catch the scammers".
- **Mobile number takeover**, 7 Apr 2026, https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/about-us/news-and-alerts/scam-alert-watch-out-for-mobile-fraud. The criminal takes the phone number. Signs: codes you did not request, telco account change alerts, the phone dropping to "SOS only".
- Also listed on the Scamwatch alerts page (titles and dates seen, bodies not opened): ATO and myGov impersonation (26 Jun 2026), job recruiters impersonating big brands (20 May 2026), fake crypto platforms pushed in "stock tips" groups (19 May 2026), food delivery impersonation (12 Jun 2026).
- **Not verified** at a primary: "press 1" bank robocalls, toll-road texts, "Hi Mum" volume in 2026.

### HK

Sources: HK Government press release "Law and order situation in Hong Kong in 2025", 11 Feb 2026, https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202602/11/P2026021100763.htm ; Secretary for Security media session on first half 2026, 31 Jul 2026 (Chinese only), https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202607/31/P2026073100870.htm

- 2025: 43,212 deception cases, about HKD 8.1b lost, 48.5% of all crime.
- By cases: e-shopping 12,505 (concert-ticket fraud 2,805, up 61.8%), telephone deception 8,621 (of which "impersonating customer service" 4,440), online investment 5,135, online employment 4,095 (96.5% click farming, "advance payments to boost sales figures"), compensated dating 1,122, phishing 1,093.
- By loss: online investment HKD 3.58b, 44.1% of all losses, average about HKD 700,000 a case. One third involved virtual assets. Many victims installed fake investment apps. In 80.4% of investment cases first contact was through mainstream social media or messaging apps.
- Elderly: "Guess Who" phone calls (the caller makes you guess which friend or relative is calling) rose 58.1% in cases.
- First half 2026: 20,613 deception cases, HKD 3.51b. Online job and investment fraud fell 22% to 74%. Phishing was the only major type that rose, up 61%. Tactics named: impersonating a government department or a bank, a same-day deadline with account cancellation as the threat, and a free rebate that needs identity verification on a fake site. Phishing losses fell 29% to about HKD 41.4m: the Secretary describes a shift to small amounts, many times.
- Hotline 18222 (ADCC). Scameter+ checks numbers, links and accounts.
- **Not verified** at a primary: impersonation of mainland officials in 2025 figures, deepfake video conferences in 2025 to 2026.

### IN

Sources: PIB, MHA reply in Lok Sabha, 10 Dec 2024, https://pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2082761 ; PIB, NHRC open house on digital arrest, 9 Jun 2026, https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2270875 ; PIB, I4C "Boss Scam" alert, 7 Aug 2026, https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2295889

- NHRC chair, quoting I4C data: about INR 52,976 crore lost to cyber-enabled fraud over six years, nearly 8% to digital arrest. NCRB keeps no separate digital-arrest series (MHA, Dec 2024). No official 2025 ranking by scam type was found.
- **Digital arrest** (MHA, Dec 2024): impersonation of state police, NCB, CBI, RBI and other agencies. International calls spoofed to show Indian mobile numbers are used in fake digital arrests and courier ("FedEx") scams. I4C blocked over 1,700 Skype IDs and 59,000 WhatsApp accounts used for digital arrest.
- **Boss scam** (I4C, Aug 2026): a zip file on WhatsApp, SMS or email named like an account statement or a regulator notice (RBI, Ministry of Corporate Affairs), with a very short compliance deadline. Opening it on a Windows computer hijacks WhatsApp Web. The account then forwards the same file to all contacts, asking them to pass it to their "finance manager for verification". Final stage: the real WhatsApp account of a senior executive, or a number saved as "CEO" on the hacked device, tells finance staff to make urgent transfers to mule accounts. Some emails impersonate the Income Tax Department.
- Real I4C alerts come from the SMS header I4CMHA-G (a useful real-twin shape).
- **Not verified** at a primary: task-scam and investment-group mechanics for 2025 to 2026, APK wedding-invite files, call-forwarding codes, sextortion volume.

### JP

Source: National Police Agency, confirmed 2025 figures (確定値), https://www.npa.go.jp/bureau/criminal/souni/tokusyusagi/sagi_keihatsu2025.pdf ; provisional page 13 Feb 2026, https://www.npa.go.jp/bureau/safetylife/sos47/new-topics/260213/03.html . Figures below are from the confirmed PDF.

- 2025: special fraud 27,832 cases, JPY 142.31b; SNS investment and romance fraud 15,168 cases, JPY 183.43b. Both record highs.
- **Fake police fraud** (ニセ警察詐欺): 11,014 cases, JPY 100.5b, 70.6% of special fraud losses. Callers claim to be police and demand money in the name of an investigation. Bank transfer is about 80% of its cases (8,771 of 11,014) and about 61% of its loss (JPY 61.26b); internet banking losses are about three times ATM losses. Elderly people who never used internet banking are walked through opening and setting it up. Gold bars: 163 cases, JPY 5.82b, mailed or collected at home. First contact: mobile phone for under 40s (over 90%), landline for 50 plus (about 60%).
- Numbers: international numbers were 75.5% of numbers used in special fraud. Recon calls asking about assets and banks: 331,653, up 71.4%.
- **SNS investment**: 9,523 cases, JPY 128.8b. First contact mostly banner ads, then direct messages (DMs up 66.4%). Instagram first, YouTube up about 21 times. Ads use celebrity images or video without permission since 2025. About 90% of ad and DM contacts are moved into a LINE "investment group". Crypto transfer cases up about 2.5 times.
- **SNS romance**: 5,645 cases, JPY 54.64b. 92.8% start by direct message; dating apps 32.7%, Instagram 22.8%, Facebook 18.6%. Crypto transfer cases up 177%.
- **Not verified** at a primary: video calls on LINE with a uniformed fake officer (the NPA page that would describe it was not opened), the "+1" prefix specifically.

### SG

Source: SPF Mid-Year Scam and Cybercrime Brief 2026 (Jan to Jun 2026), https://www.police.gov.sg/-/media/SPF/Media-Room/Statistics/Mid-Year-Scam-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2026/Mid-Year-Scam-and-Cybercrime-Brief-2026.pdf ; ScamShield page on government official impersonation, last updated 24 Jun 2026, https://www.scamshield.gov.sg/i-want-protection-from-scams/learn-to-recognise-scams/government-officials-impersonation-scams/

- 1H 2026: 16,821 scam cases, SGD 410.6m. Median loss SGD 1,350. 80.8% were self-effected transfers.
- By cases: e-commerce 3,865, phishing 3,104, investment 2,256, job 2,247, government official impersonation 1,363, social media impersonation 587, sexual services 478, loans 384, internet love 373, fake friend call 353.
- By loss: investment SGD 169.8m (avg 75,267), government official impersonation 90.8m (avg 66,643), business email compromise 57.3m (up 193%), job 34.9m, phishing 9.6m.
- **Government official impersonation**: 94.2% start with a call from someone posing as a bank, telco or financial institution about a suspicious card transaction, a new phone line or an unpaid bill. When the victim denies it, the call is transferred to a fake MAS, Ministry of Law or police officer who alleges money laundering. Asks: transfer to a "safety account", ATM cash deposits, hand over cash, gold or luxury watches to a courier, or hand over the ATM card and PIN. Video calls on WhatsApp; victims are asked to show ID and bank cards on camera. At least 30 cases used VoIP. ScamShield: officials never transfer your call to the police, never ask for bank logins or app installs. Scammers may already know your name and ID number.
- **Investment**: ads offering free tips, impersonating a university or a brokerage, collect contact details, then add the person to a chat group styled as an "investment learning community". A "mentor" posts picks; fake members vouch. Then transfers to bank accounts or crypto wallets, sometimes cash handovers, or accounts on fake apps that may be downloaded from official app stores and show fake growth. Withdrawals meet "fees" or "taxes".
- **E-commerce**: Carousell and Telegram lead. Top item is collectible trading cards, driven by pre-orders: a deposit or full payment now, delivery months away. Seller-side variant: fake buyers send fake payment screenshots.
- **Social media impersonation**: 66.6% (391 cases) came from a contact's **compromised** chat account, not a new number. Reasons: an emergency loan or a bank transfer problem. Payment to bank or payment-service accounts. Repeat transfers asked after the first.
- **Business email compromise**: spoofed or compromised supplier email asks to change the bank account, with real letterhead, signature and stamp. Variant: a fake CEO email asks for the staff member's mobile number, moves to WhatsApp, and asks for gift cards bought in a shop, scratched, with the codes sent over.
- **Phishing**: iMessage from random email addresses or overseas numbers, posing as a courier, "invalid delivery address", a small re-delivery fee, card details and the bank token approval; the card is then added to a phone wallet.
- **Job scams**: home-based tasks (rating hotels, reviewing apps, liking posts, advance purchases). Small commission first, then pay to unlock. "Social media boosting" tasks are the growing variant.
- Contact methods: messaging 27.6%, social media 26.7%, shopping platforms 11.2%, phone 9.7%. Since July 2024 government SMS come from one gov.sg sender ID.

### Cross-country patterns that are verified

1. **Two-stage authority calls.** A service desk first (bank, telco, courier), then a "transfer" to an officer (SG verified; MY and JP consistent but not verified as two-stage).
2. **Chat-group investment funnels** from ads, fake news pages or hobby groups, moved to a chat group with a mentor and fake members (SG, AU, JP, HK).
3. **Compromised real accounts** are now the sender, not only new numbers (SG social media impersonation; IN boss scam).
4. **No link at all**: the callback receipt pushes a phone call (AU).
5. **Small amounts, many times** for phishing (HK 2026).
6. **Crypto and cash handover** rising as the payment ask (JP, SG, HK).
7. **Fake apps from official stores**, not only side-loaded files (SG, HK).

## 3. Live card sample, 16 Oct 2026

32 public cards, four per pack, from `/api/day?age=adult&quick=0`. Half are real twins by design.

### What reads real
- Bank-check SMS (HK, ID, JP): short, institution-prefixed, account-block threat, link. This is the right register.
- Seller refund card (MY): moving off the marketplace, screenshot proof, overpayment to a relative's account. Close to the SG verified pattern.
- Paid-task card (SG): small credited trial, deposit to unlock. Right shape.

### What makes cards read fake
1. **Amounts.** "500 IDR", "500 JPY", "500 INR" for an urgent bill. IDR 500 and JPY 500 are coins. Most cards use 500 whatever the currency, because the Director prompt says "short figures like 500" and never receives the stake.
2. **Same family-trouble script in seven of eight days.** Same beats in nearly the same words: the broken-phone excuse, the no-calls excuse, the secrecy line. `server/deal.ts` sets `FIRST_TRICK = 'family_trouble'` and deals it first on first play, so this one script is what every first-time player sees.
3. **Tell stuffing.** Every tell is spelled out in the ask: an identity reassurance, a secrecy line, a deadline in minutes and a named new payee, all in two parts. Real messages carry one or two pressures and leave the rest structural.
4. **Real twins recite the lesson.** Twins say what they do not ask for: "No payment or signature is required", "We never charge any application fees", and one twin says law enforcement never asks for transfers. Real messages never do this, and it gives away which card is safe.
5. **Strangers use the player's people's names.** A recruiter introduces themselves as Lena from a hiring firm while Lena is also the player's sister on the next card. The Director's name rule forces every sender to come from the allowed list.
6. **Romance register is parody.** Declarations like "soulmate" in the first message, and the camera excuse repeated in every part.
7. **"The practice code on screen" inside scam text.** It breaks the fiction mid-message.
8. **Doubled sender names.** "Meridian Prize Desk Rewards", "Civic Notice Desk Information Desk".
9. **Wrong institution type or channel.** A police "informational notice" arriving on a green chat app as a real twin (IN). Real notices come by letter or the agency's own channel.
10. **Length and grammar.** Many parts are full, polite, grammatical sentences of 20 to 30 words. Scam texts are short, template-shaped and slightly off.

Note, not a bug: `bannerHint: likely_scam` appears on some real twins. `server/deal.ts` draws it at 1 in 6 for twins on purpose.

## 4. The ten edits that would most raise realism, ranked

1. **Amounts by currency and stake.** `server/agents/director.ts`, `userMessage` and `DIRECTOR_SYSTEM`. Pass `stakeFor(pack.walletStart)` and the currency. Replace "short figures like 500" with: amounts between about a tenth and a third of the stake band, written as people write that currency (round thousands for IDR and JPY). Keep the four-digit cap by allowing "2 juta", "30k" style or by raising the cap for currency figures only if the Guard allows it.
2. **Stop tell stuffing.** `director.ts`, the rule that every note tell must be worded in the ask. Allow a tell to be carried by structure (a new payee, a new number, a familiar account asking something unusual). Allow urgency as "today", "before tonight", "before the courier leaves", not only minutes.
3. **Real twins never mention what they do not ask for.** `director.ts`, the real-twin rule. Add: the twin is an ordinary message; it never states "no fee", "no payment", "we never ask". It ends when its business ends.
4. **Register rule.** `director.ts`. Scam parts are short (under about 15 words each), template-shaped, often lower polish, one pressure per part. Romance builds over a few small messages, not declarations. No literary words.
5. **Stranger names.** `director.ts`, `namesFor` and the name rule. Split the list: the player's people for family and friend cards; the neutral pool or an invented business sender for strangers. A recruiter never carries a family member's name.
6. **Family-trouble variants.** Note `family-trouble`. Add the compromised-account variant (familiar number, unusual ask) and a follow-up second ask. Rotate variants by seed so the first-play card is not the same script in every pack.
7. **Two-stage authority call.** `scripts/tts-beats.ts` (the beat prompt that writes the six beats), with the caller identity in `server/agents/caller.ts`. Make beat 1 a service desk (card charge, new line, unpaid bill) and add a hand-off line to "an officer". Treat the transfer itself as a tell in the lesson. `caller.ts` only picks beats, so the change is in the beat script. Note `digital-arrest` gets the same two-stage setup and the cash or card handover ask.
8. **Callback variant, no link.** Notes `bank-check` and `refund-payment`. Add a receipt for an unrecognised charge with a number to call. The unsafe action is calling the printed number. This teaches a 2026 pattern that has no link to inspect.
9. **Investment entry points.** Note `investment-group`. Add the free-tips ad impersonating a university or broker, the fake news page, and the hobby group; the app installs from the normal store; small early withdrawal.
10. **Supplier account change.** Note `payment-change`. Lead with the email from a known supplier asking to update bank details, then the boss-on-chat gift-card variant. Keep the deepfake video as level 3 only.

Smaller fixes: drop "the practice code on screen" from scam text in `DIRECTOR_SYSTEM` and say "the code just sent to you" (the code moment stays a button); fix doubled sender names where `server/deal.ts` fills the institution into a display name that already has one; the IN real twin for digital arrest should arrive by letter or the agency's own app, not a chat app.
